# Food & Beverage — Uganda Context

## Market Overview
- Food vending is one of the most common and accessible businesses in refugee settlements
- Types: cooked food stalls (rolex, chapati, rice & beans), brewing (local beverages), bakery/snacks, fresh juice
- Very low entry barrier — often just a cooking stove, charcoal, and basic ingredients
- Highly competitive but consistent demand (everyone needs to eat)
- Location near schools, markets, health centers, NGO offices drives volume

## Benchmarks
- **Cooked food stall**: Gross margins 40-60%. A plate of food costs UGX 1,500-2,500 to make, sells for UGX 3,000-5,000. A busy stall serves 50-150 customers/day.
- **Chapati/rolex**: Cost per unit ~UGX 500-800, sell for UGX 1,000-2,000. High volume, thin margins.
- **Local brewing**: Margins 50-70%. Low material cost. Main inputs: sorghum, millet, sugar. Regulatory risks in some areas.
- **Bakery/snacks**: Margins 40-55%. Mandazi, samosa, baked goods. Shelf life limits production scale.
- **Fresh juice/drinks**: Margins 50-65%. Seasonal fruit availability affects costs.

## Supply Chain
- Daily purchase of fresh ingredients from local markets
- Charcoal or firewood as primary cooking fuel (UGX 2,000-5,000/day)
- Some operators buy in bulk weekly from Gulu/Adjumani town markets
- Water access can be a significant cost factor (UGX 500-1,000 per jerrycan in some areas)

## Common Challenges
- Food safety and hygiene perceptions affecting customer trust
- Perishability — unsold food is wasted daily
- Fuel costs (charcoal) can be 15-25% of revenue
- Health and licensing requirements (food handling certificates)
- Price sensitivity — customers resist price increases even when input costs rise
- Rainy season reduces foot traffic

## Opportunities
- Catering contracts with NGOs, schools, and events (higher margins, more predictable)
- Diversifying menu to include higher-margin items (fresh juice, specialty dishes)
- Breakfast and lunch service targeting different customer segments at different times
- Delivery to workplaces and offices via boda-boda
- Value-added products: bottled sauces, dried spice mixes, packaged snacks for retail
- Loyalty programs: meal cards or punch cards for regular customers
- Social media marketing: WhatsApp status photos of daily menu to build regular customers
