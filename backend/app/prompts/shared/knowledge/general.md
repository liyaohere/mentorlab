# General Business Knowledge — Uganda & East Africa Context

## Common Benchmarks for Early-Stage Businesses
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC): varies by sector. Rule of thumb: CAC should be less than 1/3 of the customer's lifetime value
- Monthly revenue growth: 10-20% month-over-month is strong for early-stage
- Gross margins by sector: services 50-70%, retail 15-30%, food/beverage 40-60%, agriculture (raw) 30-50%, agriculture (processed) 50-70%
- Break-even: most small businesses in East Africa take 6-18 months to reach profitability
- Working capital: plan for 2-3 months of operating expenses as buffer

## Key Business Frameworks (Simple Versions)
- **Unit economics**: How much do you earn from each sale minus the cost to make/serve it? If this number is positive, you have a viable business at the unit level.
- **Product-market fit signals**: Customers come back without being asked. They tell friends. They're willing to pay before you deliver. They complain when you're closed.
- **Growth levers**: Only five ways to grow — raise prices, sell more to each customer, sell more often to each customer, find new customers, add new products/services.
- **80/20 rule**: Often 20% of your products or customers generate 80% of your profit. Know which ones.

## East African Business Context
- **Mobile money** (MTN Mobile Money, Airtel Money) is the primary payment method. Cash is common for small transactions. Bank accounts are rare among small business owners.
- **Trust and relationships** are foundational. Business often follows personal connections. Reputation in the community is your most valuable asset.
- **Seasonal patterns**: Agriculture (harvest seasons), education (term dates for school-related businesses), holidays (Christmas, Eid), weather (rainy season reduces foot traffic).
- **Cross-border trade**: East African Community allows relatively free movement of goods. South Sudan and DRC border trade is significant for northern Uganda.
- **Common challenges**: Access to capital (microfinance rates of 2-4% per month), unreliable power, poor roads (especially during rain), regulatory complexity, limited market information.
- **Digital opportunities**: WhatsApp is the dominant business communication tool. Facebook is common for marketing. Smartphone penetration is growing but data costs matter.

## Refugee Business Context
- Refugee entrepreneurs face additional challenges: limited land rights, work permit restrictions (though Uganda has relatively progressive refugee policies), social barriers with host communities, and distance from major markets.
- Refugee settlements in northern Uganda (Palabek, Adjumani, Bidi Bidi, Rhino Camp) are semi-urban with trading centers and weekly markets.
- NGO presence creates both demand (for goods and services) and competition (free handouts can undercut businesses).
- Remittances from diaspora connections can be a source of startup capital.
- Skills from home countries (DRC, South Sudan, Somalia, Burundi) can be competitive advantages if adapted to local market.
