# Services — Uganda Context

## Market Overview
- Common service businesses in refugee settlements: tailoring/sewing, hairdressing/barbering, phone repair, motorcycle (boda-boda) transport, construction labor, catering/events
- Service businesses typically require less startup capital than retail
- Key advantage: skills-based, harder to replicate than a retail shop
- Strong word-of-mouth economy — reputation is everything
- Seasonal demand patterns: construction peaks in dry season, tailoring peaks before holidays/school terms

## Benchmarks by Service Type
- **Tailoring**: Margins 50-70%. A dress costs UGX 10,000-30,000 in materials, sells for UGX 25,000-80,000. School uniform orders are the most reliable income.
- **Hairdressing/Barbering**: Margins 60-80%. Low material cost per customer. A haircut is UGX 2,000-5,000. Braiding UGX 10,000-50,000. The constraint is time and chairs.
- **Phone repair**: Margins 40-60%. Common repairs (screen, charging port) cost UGX 5,000-15,000 in parts, charge UGX 15,000-40,000.
- **Boda-boda**: Daily revenue UGX 30,000-60,000. Fuel cost UGX 10,000-15,000/day. Motorcycle maintenance ~UGX 100,000/month.
- **Catering/Events**: Margins 30-50% for events. Per-plate cost UGX 3,000-5,000, charge UGX 8,000-15,000.

## Customer Acquisition
- Word of mouth is the dominant channel (70%+ of new customers)
- Physical signage at shop/workspace location
- WhatsApp status updates showing recent work (especially tailoring, hairdressing)
- Community events and church/mosque gatherings for networking
- Referral incentives (discount for customers who bring new clients)

## Common Challenges
- Irregular demand — feast or famine cycle
- Difficulty scaling beyond personal capacity (hiring and training apprentices)
- Power outages affecting service delivery (tailoring, hairdressing, phone repair)
- Tool and equipment maintenance costs
- Customers expecting credit or delayed payment

## Opportunities
- Training apprentices to serve more customers (leverage labor)
- Expanding from single service to related services (e.g., barbershop adds phone charging, tailoring adds fabric sales)
- Building a booking/queue system via phone to reduce customer wait times
- Targeting institutional clients (NGOs, schools, churches) for larger, more reliable contracts
- Mobile/home service delivery for customers who can't easily travel
